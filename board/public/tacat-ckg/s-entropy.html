00 · The foundation

Distance to the truth is a property of the observer, not of the answer.

This is where the rest comes from, and it is the most abstract page here. Everything else — the floor, the medium vertex, the cut, path opacity — is this calculus applied in one domain or another. It is also the part most in need of repair, and this page says where.

Read this page last, or skip it. The results on the other pages do not depend on accepting any of this. The instrumental floor is arithmetic from a datasheet; the cofactor split is checkable against a reaction database; the min-cut is standard graph theory. This page explains where the ideas came from, which is a different question from whether they hold.

The receiver

The move that starts everything: do not measure distance in the space of answers. Measure it from a stated observer. A receiver is a four-part object — a signal space, a decoder, an internal knowledge framework, and a projection back onto candidate answers. It is bounded when its internal state set is strictly smaller than the space of candidates.

Given a receiver, an S-value is how far that receiver stands from the truth on a given candidate, on a bounded scale. Not a distance between two answers — a distance mediated by the machinery doing the knowing.

Residue persistence — the result that holds

Proved, and correctly

If a receiver's internal capacity is strictly smaller than the total information space, then the region it cannot reach has the cardinality of the whole space. Not a shrinking remainder — the same size as what it started with. The proof is three lines of cardinal arithmetic and it is right.

This is the honest foundation. A bounded knower in a larger space has an irreducible blind region, and its size does not diminish as the knower improves. Everything about floors, smallest knowable errors, and why individuation costs something should be built on this.

The floor — and a defect in how it was derived

The framework states that no bounded receiver reaches zero: there is a smallest knowable error, characteristic of the receiver rather than of the truth. The intuition is right and the consequences are useful. The derivation, in the recursion paper, is circular.

The circularity, stated plainly. Axiom S3 already asserts that a floor function exists with a strictly positive value for every receiver. The theorem then proves that the floor is strictly positive — which is a hypothesis of the axiom system, not a consequence of it. The proof also opens “suppose the floor is zero; by Axiom S3 there is a candidate at zero”, but Axiom S3 is precisely what forbids that. The final step invokes “positive measure with respect to any cardinality-preserving measure”, and cardinality does not induce a measure.
The repair, and it is small

Weaken Axiom S3 to assert only that a floor function exists, and derive its positivity from residue persistence. The floor then becomes a definition built on a sound theorem rather than an assumption re-proved. Everything downstream — why linear justification fails, why validation must be circular — survives unchanged, now on a foundation that holds.

Two other papers reach a floor by better routes, and both are on other pages. The instrumental floor is D/N from a datasheet (the floor); the graph floor is the minimum weight of a realisable separator (individuation). Neither needs Axiom S3.

Information catalysts — the cleanest result in the framework

A catalyst is an expression whose application strictly reduces the receiver's distance to truth. Its catalytic power is the fraction of the remaining above-floor distance it removes — a number in [0, 1], where 0 is neutral and 1 closes the gap in one step.

Multiplicativity of catalytic power

Compose two catalysts and the powers do not add. The residuals multiply:

κ(γ₁ ∘ γ₂) = 1 − (1 − κ₁)(1 − κ₂)

A chemist recognises that shape immediately: it is how independent efficiencies compose, and it is why a cascade of good-but-imperfect steps has a predictable end-to-end yield. The corollary is the familiar one — repeating a catalyst n times gives 1 − (1 − κ)ⁿ, which approaches 1 and never arrives.

Unconstrained subtasks — what it does and does not say

An expression resolving to a global value admits arbitrary decomposition into subtasks — signed, fractional, type-mixed, even locally impossible — provided the composition still evaluates to that value. A subtask may sit at maximum distance from the truth while the whole sits at the floor.

The proof is short, and it should be. If the evaluation map is a homomorphism, substituting an equivalent sub-expression preserves the whole. That is compositionality, true in any algebra, and the worked examples all amount to adding a term and its negation. So the theorem is correct and it is not surprising — what makes it interesting is not the algebra but what it licenses: the interior of a computation is free when only the endpoint is checked. That page states the version worth defending, along with the condition that is still missing.

Triple equivalence and recursive coordinates

Two further structural claims complete the calculus. Triple equivalence holds that oscillatory, categorical and partition representations of a state are convertible, so a computation can be relocated to whichever representation makes it cheapest — the ancestor of representation mobility in the chemistry language. And no privileged level holds that every S-value decomposes into a triple at the next depth, each itself a global value of its sub-problem, with the same laws at every scale — the ancestor of addressing a computation at any depth.

Both are load-bearing for the architecture and both are proved by construction with trivial witnesses. Treat them as design commitments that the implementation then justifies, rather than as discoveries.

Why this page exists at all

Because the honest answer to “where does this come from?” is better than a vague one, and because the defects above are ones a careful reader would find. The strongest version of the framework is the one that says: residue persistence is sound, the catalyst algebra is clean and useful, the floor should be defined from the first rather than assumed, and the subtask theorem is a compositionality result whose value lies in what it licenses downstream.
