01 · Individuation

A part is fixed as the complement of everything it is not.

Nothing in a space is distinguished in advance. To pick out a part you must either point at something already distinguished — which begs the question — or say what the part is not. Individuation by negation is the only route that does not regress, and it has a consequence: every part carries a boundary, and the boundary has weight.

Put the surroundings in the graph

The move that makes this computable is small. Take the contact graph of a system — atoms, residues, species as vertices; contacts as weighted edges — and add one distinguished vertex m, the medium, adjacent to everything. The medium is what the system is told apart from: the solution, the bath, the surroundings.

Now “the identity of v” has a definition rather than a convention. It is the minimum cut separating v from m — the cheapest way to sever that part from its surroundings. Two numbers fall out: the weight of that cut, and the size of the minimising side.

Why a vertex and not an attribute

Conventionally the solvent is a property attached to a reaction record. As a vertex it participates in the structure, so questions about it become computations rather than lookups. Without a fixed second terminal, “the minimum cut of v” is not even well posed.

Consequence: solvent role is computed, not annotated

A solvent molecule is structural when the protein holds it more tightly than the solution does — when its cut against the host clears the cut against the medium — and bulk otherwise. That is the ordinary chemical criterion. The point is not that it is novel; the point is that the framework can now compute it, so “what is the role of the solvent in this reaction?” has an answer of the form

structural, because ρstr = 1.48 × 10⁻³ ≥ w(·, m) = 3.70 × 10⁻⁴ measured, medium-vertex validation, exp01

derived from the graph, with no curator supplying it and no controlled-vocabulary term recording it. An ordered active-site water and a bulk water stop being two instances of one class and become different kinds of object.

Where this reads backwards, and why. The theorem says that making a solvent species more abundant makes its bound instances more readily structural. On first encounter that is the wrong way round. The resolution is that the comparison is about discriminability, not about binding: against a more abundant background, an individually held instance stands out further. A reader who rejects that reading should reject the naming, not the mathematics — and the naming is fair to criticise.

Consequence: direction belongs to the medium

A residue chain and its reverse commit identical total boundary. Same cuts, same count, same committed edges — summation is commutative. So the cut structure alone cannot orient a process. Whatever makes a reaction run one way is not in the reaction.

What orients it is which end the medium holds depleted. Define the medium bias as the cost of individuating the products minus the cost of individuating the reactants, and a trichotomy follows immediately: forward-irreversible, reverse-irreversible, or undirected in this medium.

Consequence: two views disagree about the cofactor, structurally

Take the transaminases. In the signature view — the one a reaction database records — a participant is an entity bearing a role in the net transformation. Pyridoxal phosphate is regenerated: modified in the first half-reaction, restored in the second. Its net coefficient is zero, it bears no net role, and including it would unbalance the equation. Rhea accordingly records four participants for each of the three reactions, and none of them is PLP.

In the cut-chain view — the one a mechanism needs — a process is a sequence of individuations. PLP carries the amino group between the half-reactions. Delete it and the mechanism is gone, because the two halves connect through it and through nothing else.

The finding

The two verdicts are about different predicates: bears a net role in the transformation and is individuated in the process. The first is false of PLP; the second is true. This is not an incompleteness of either view. It is a representational partition — and it is why a single ontology cannot answer both question groups.

The practical reading: the recommendation of linked micro-ontologies is not a workaround for insufficient effort at unification. It is the empirical shadow of a partition that is there in the structure, and this gives a mechanism for it.
