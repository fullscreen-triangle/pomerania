The chemistry

The inverse of the other chapters. You know the content; what this adds is why each piece of chemistry forced a modelling decision — and which ones nobody else competing will have got right.

★★★ your advantage 15 min 2 things to click the best 30 seconds you have

1. The three reactions

Three PLP-dependent aminotransferases, each moving an amino group from an L-amino-acid donor onto the shared acceptor 2-oxoglutarate, yielding L-glutamate and the corresponding 2-oxo acid.

ECEnzymeRhea masterReaction
2.6.1.1aspartate transaminase21824 L-aspartate + 2-oxoglutarate ⇌ oxaloacetate + L-glutamate
2.6.1.2alanine transaminase19453 L-alanine + 2-oxoglutarate ⇌ pyruvate + L-glutamate
2.6.1.3cysteine transaminase17441 L-cysteine + 2-oxoglutarate ⇌ 2-oxo-3-sulfanylpropanoate + L-glutamate

The shared acceptor is what makes a cross-cutting question possible at all. It also makes "substrate specificity" a question about the amino donor rather than the substrate set as a whole — which is why CQ03 reads the way it does.

Why the choice of enzyme is not incidental

LARA — the assessor's lab-automation platform — describes its purpose as "fully automatized transaminase high throughput protein screening." These are not three arbitrary reactions from BRENDA; they are what his robots screen.

Transaminases make chiral amines, which is what pharma wants them for, and the reversibility that makes them synthetically awkward is precisely the reversibility that makes direction modelling non-trivial. The chemistry and the modelling difficulty are the same fact. That connection is free to make and almost nobody else will make it.

And the one that follows from it — the definition cannot classify his enzyme

The transaminases LARA screens are ω-transaminases — amine transaminases. They run amine + keto acid → ketone + amino acid. There is no amino-acid donor and no 2-oxo-acid product, so two of the four conjuncts fail and the reasoner does not classify them.

The three shipped reactions are α-transaminations, so the model fits what it models. What overclaims is the class name. Answer with the fix, not an apology: "α-transamination is what I defined. A hierarchy is the fix — α and ω as siblings under a parent requiring only the amino-group transfer — not a wider definition."

This is the most likely hard question in the whole project, precisely because the chapter above is right that these are his daily subject matter.

2. Chemical identity is harder than it looks

You know L-glutamate at pH 7.4 is not glutamic acid. What is worth knowing is that ChEBI encodes that distinction as separate entries, and Rhea uses the microspecies exclusively.

The root cause is more interesting than the error

v0 did not guess these. Its own documentation states the method: "ChEBI, resolved via KEGG's conv/chebi cross-reference." KEGG's mapping returns the neutral parent.

That is the better story: not "I typed the wrong number" but "a defensible automated cross- reference introduced a systematic bias, and only checking against the primary source caught it." Worse, v0's own formula and charge fields were already the correct microspecies values — so its balance tests passed on data that did not belong to the identifiers printed beside it.

Trap 1 — never resolve a ChEBI identifier from a name

The bare accepted name "L-cysteine" belongs to CHEBI:17561 — a tautomer, not the species these reactions use (35235). Searching by name gives a confident, well-formed, wrong answer.

Trap 2 — ChEBI's names differ from what Rhea prints

ChEBI says L-alanine zwitterion; Rhea's equation says L-alanine. Three of nine diverge. The project records both in a NAME_DIVERGENCES table rather than silently picking one.

Trap 3 — the character that breaks joins

ChEBI's charge suffixes use U+2212 MINUS SIGN (−) while the L- prefix is an ASCII hyphen (-). The same string contains both. A naive join between scraped text and a database dump fails silently, and the two characters are visually almost identical. The linter is configured to stop "correcting" it, with a comment explaining why.

3. PLP — the detail that proves the domain knowledge

You know this cold. What matters is why it is the single most diagnostic decision in the submission.

Why this is the diagnostic detail

Model PLP as a fourth substrate and every check still passes: the graph validates, the shapes are satisfied, the queries run, the container builds. The only thing wrong is that the stoichiometry is a lie — and no amount of software engineering will surface it.

It is the error a biochemist spots instantly and a software engineer never would. There is a test, test_plp_is_not_a_reaction_participant, asserting it never appears as one. Note also that CQ04 answers mechanism and cofactor together — because PLP hangs off the enzyme precisely because the mechanism is ping-pong bi-bi. The two facts are one fact.

4. Direction: substrate and product are roles

L-glutamate is a product in all three transaminations and a substrate in glutamate dehydrogenase. So "is L-glutamate a substrate?" has no answer — it depends on the reaction and the direction.

That is why participation had to become a node with a role on it, and it is what makes the CQ05/CQ06 pair possible. But the sharpest evidence is external. Switch organism:

The best thirty seconds available in the presentation

Same reaction. Same identifier. Opposite roles. That is not a modelling opinion — it is an annotated fact in UniProt with experimental evidence, and it proves the role model answers a real question rather than a manufactured one.

Rhea's four identifiers

Rhea mints four IDs per reaction at consecutive offsets: an undirected master, then left-to-right, right-to-left, and bidirectional children. UniProt's Reaction= line always cites the master; PhysiologicalDirection= cites a child. Treating them as interchangeable mixes "this reaction" with "this reaction, in this direction". The project anchors on masters and carries the children explicitly.

The counter-argument to be ready for

If the master is undirected, is it right for the graph to say a reaction produces L-glutamate? Arguably it should only say the reactions share it.

Checked against the live endpoint: RHEA:19453 does carry 19453_L and 19453_R sides and a canonically written equation. So the master is undirected about physiological direction — that is what the children are for — while still having a left and a right. Assigning roles per the written orientation is consistent with it, and CQ06 is worded "in the left-to-right orientation" for exactly this reason.

The residual is real and worth conceding, and it survived the move to rdf:type: our reaction is an instance of the undirected master class, and it carries roles in an orientation that class does not itself assert. The honest answer is the paragraph above, not a retreat.

5. The negative control, chosen chemically

Glutamate dehydrogenase (EC 1.4.1.2): L-glutamate + H₂O + NAD⁺ ⇌ 2-oxoglutarate + NH₄⁺ + NADH.

Chosen because it is the hardest available negative. It contains both headline molecules of all three transaminations, roles exactly reversed, and it is a real enzyme doing real chemistry on the same metabolites — not a strawman. A definition keyed on molecular content would classify it; a definition keyed on roles does not.

Choosing it required knowing the metabolism, not the software. The classifier in Chapter 2 lets you watch it fail and then pass.

6. One place where the data and the model disagree

Know this before you are asked

reference_data.py records — correctly — that in mammals EC 2.6.1.3 is not a distinct gene product: it is a secondary activity of GOT1 (P17174), the same protein carrying EC 2.6.1.1. Three reactions, two proteins.

The ontology nevertheless mints three separate Enzyme individuals, keyed on EC number. So the data layer knows something the model does not express.

Raise it yourself — it shows you can see the seam between "what my source data knows" and "what my model can say", which is the same skill the whole exercise tests.

7. What to be able to say

The 30-second version

"Two chemical decisions drove the model. PLP hangs off the enzyme, not the reaction, because it is a prosthetic group that cycles PLP→PMP→PLP bound to an active-site lysine — which is why Rhea's equations have four participants and UniProt records it as a COFACTOR on the protein. Modelling it as a fourth substrate would validate perfectly and be chemically false. And substrate versus product is a role in a direction, not a property of a molecule — E. coli AlaA carries the same Rhea master as human ALT1 but runs right-to-left, consuming the glutamate ALT1 produces. That is why participation is an n-ary node carrying a role."
