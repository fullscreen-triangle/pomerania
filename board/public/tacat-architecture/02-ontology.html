The ontology

Sheet 01 showed schema.py as one box. This is what is inside it: eight classes, nine properties, and one defined class that the reasoner uses to recognise transaminations rather than merely check them.

Two modelling decisions carry most of the weight, and both are visible in the first diagram: participation goes through a bridge node because RDF triples are binary, and the cofactor hangs off the enzyme rather than the reaction because that is where the chemistry puts it.

Participation is an n-ary relation

A reaction relates an entity, a role and a coefficient — three things at once. A binary hasSubstrate has nowhere to put the last two, so participation goes through a ReactionParticipant node that carries all three. This is the W3C n-ary relation pattern, Use Case 1; BioPAX calls its equivalent an "n-ary bridge" in the ontology's own rdfs:comment, and UniProt reaches for the same construct with up:Catalytic_Activity.

One reaction, four bridge nodes. Model PLP as a fifth participant and the graph still validates, the queries still run, and the stoichiometry is a lie — which is why test_plp_is_not_a_reaction_participant exists.

That shape is what makes this pair of questions answerable, and different: which reactions have L-glutamate as a participant (four, including the control), versus which reactions produce it (three). Role is a property of the participation, not of the molecule.

The defined class, and the control that must not match

TransaminationReaction is declared with equivalent_to — necessary and sufficient conditions — over chemical classes and participant roles, never over specific molecules. Nothing is ever asserted to be a transamination; HermiT derives all three at build time. 

class TransaminationReaction(BiochemicalReaction):
    equivalent_to = [
        BiochemicalReaction
        & hasParticipant.some(ReactionParticipant & participatingEntity.some(AminoAcid)  & participantRole.value(reactant))
        & hasParticipant.some(ReactionParticipant & participatingEntity.some(TwoOxoAcid) & participantRole.value(reactant))
        & hasParticipant.some(ReactionParticipant & participatingEntity.some(TwoOxoAcid) & participantRole.value(product))
        & hasParticipant.some(ReactionParticipant & participatingEntity.some(AminoAcid)  & participantRole.value(product))
    ]

The negative control is the informative half. Glutamate dehydrogenase contains both headline molecules of the transaminations — L-glutamate and 2-oxoglutarate — with their roles reversed. A definition written over molecule identity would have matched it.

The control is not obviously different — it matches half the definition. That is what makes it a real test: it fails on role, which is precisely the thing the model claims to discriminate on.

"Not derived" is not "proven false." Under the open-world assumption HermiT has failed to prove the control is a transamination; it has not proved it is not. The CLI says "not derived (roles do not match the definition)" for exactly this reason. Asserting non-membership would be a closed-world claim, and that is SHACL's job — which is why both run.

What the definition does not constrain

Worth stating plainly, because the class name is broader than its logical extension. The four conjuncts define a participant-role signature, not a mechanism. The expression says nothing about:

Not constrainedConsequence
Atom or amino-group transferNothing checks that a group actually moved.
Mass or charge balanceAn unbalanced equation still classifies.
Additional participantsAdd ATP → ADP + phosphate and it still classifies.
Stoichiometric coefficientsCoefficients 2,1,1,1 classify under OWL; only SHACL objects.
PLP dependence, or any enzymeA reaction with no catalysedBy still classifies.
The ping-pong bi-bi mechanismCarried as a datum on the enzyme, not encoded in the class.

An overstatement that was in the source, and is now fixed. The docstring in schema.py used to call these four conjuncts a complete mechanism signature. They are not — as the table above shows, the mechanism is nowhere in the class expression. It now reads the net participant-role signature shared by the three canonical amino-acid/2-oxo-acid transaminations, and spells out what the conjuncts do not constrain. Flagged by an independent review; both that fix and the AllDifferent one have landed.

Why the role individuals live in the schema. The definition restricts with participantRole.value(reactant) — a hasValue restriction on a named individual. Those individuals must exist when the class is declared, so they sit in schema.py beside the classes despite being individuals. The obvious module split would have put them in the A-Box and silently broken the definition.
