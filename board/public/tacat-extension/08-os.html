Chapter 8 · second series

What exists, and what does not

Chapters 6 and 7 described a design. This one describes its state — what runs, what is measured, what is written but not integrated, and what is only a claim. It ends with the single experiment that would show the whole design is wrong, because that experiment does not exist yet and it is the most useful thing anyone could do to this project.

★★ the honest page 9 min status · falsification · cost contains the disproof condition
Provenance. Everything here is single-developer hobby work with no users, no reviewers, and no publications. The status table in §1 is the whole of it — there is no fuller version being held back. Where something is described as running, it runs on my machine and nowhere else, which is the weakest sense of the word that is still true.

2. The one piece of evidence that is not self-report

Everything above is me describing my own work, which is worth little. There is one fact in the codebase that is not of that kind, and it is the reason I still think the abstraction is real.

The floor β — the positive lower bound on individuation cost from Chapter 1 §2 — is implemented four separate times, in three repositories, by four modules that do not import each other. I did not plan this; I noticed it afterwards while trying to unify them, and discovered they should not be unified.

ModuleHow the floor appearsWhat it is doing there
the accountability language a field on every type, statically checked A value whose floor is not positive is not typeable — the sharp cut is a compile error, not a runtime check.
purpose derived: minimum positive edge weight of the context graph The cheapest separator available. Every non-isolated step's residue is guaranteed to sit above it.
the search engine declared in the script and parsed out The author states the resolution at which the search is willing to individuate.
the scheduler a divisor guard in the priority formula Prevents a task whose residue has reached its threshold from acquiring infinite priority.
Why this is the strongest thing on the page

Four modules written months apart for unrelated jobs each needed a positive lower bound on how finely a thing can be distinguished, and each implemented it in the idiom of its own layer — a type invariant, a graph derivation, a script declaration, a division guard. Nothing coordinated that. It is the difference between an abstraction that was designed and one that was found, and it is checkable in about a minute: four files, one line each.

3. The experiment that would show this is all wrong

Chapter 7 §3 made an unusual claim: that if the three validations converge, the design is falsified rather than confirmed. That claim is testable, the test is cheap, and it has never been run.

The claim the whole design rests on untested

For a given task, the necessity decomposition, the sufficiency decomposition and the value decomposition are genuinely different partitions — different subtasks, not one subtask list scored three ways. If they are the same partition, three validations are one validation with ceremony, and two thirds of the architecture should be deleted.

The harness for this already exists — the six-experiment validation file from §1 is the right shape and the right place. The experiment is a seventh function in it:

What each outcome would mean
  • Partitions differ, residues move independently — the design survives its first real test, and the three-verdict output in Chapter 7 is worth building properly.
  • Partitions differ but residues track each other — the decompositions are real but the measurements are not independent. Salvageable, and it would say exactly which two to merge.
  • Partitions collapse — the architecture is one validation wearing three hats. Chapters 6 and 7 are wrong and should be withdrawn.

4. What this would actually be worth to a lab system, if it worked

Stated at the level it can be defended at, which is lower than the level the idea is exciting at.

What it would add

A structured answer to "can this running process be modified?" — the question CoRDI 2023 footnote 2 leaves open — in the form of three independent facts rather than one verdict.

A recorded residual per run: what the system did not establish, carried in the data alongside what it did. That is the same object as tacat:notCompared from Chapter 1, and it is a provenance claim, which is the layer this whole submission is about.

A stopping rule that is not "the checks I wrote passed" — see Chapter 2.

What it would not

Anything about devices, drivers, protocols or hardware. That is the majority of the difficulty in real lab automation and none of it is addressed.

Any improvement to throughput, reliability or cost. The three-verdict output is more work to act on, not less.

Any of it soon. Integration of the three validations over one task is the current work and it is not finished.

5. The honest summary

If asked what this is

A hobby project by one person, in integration, with a booting prototype and a measured baseline, whose central claim has never been tested. It shares a three-part architecture with LARA that neither derived from the other, and it has a specific answer to one problem LARA's own paper records as open. Whether that answer is any good depends on an experiment I have described in §3 and not run.

The reason to mention it at all is not that it is finished. It is that the correspondence in Chapter 6 §2 is specific enough to be checked, the falsification condition in §3 is stated rather than avoided, and the four-way floor convergence in §2 is a fact about the codebase rather than a claim about my intentions.

The one question worth more than all eight chapters

Does a scientific task genuinely decompose three different ways? Everything above rests on it and nothing above demonstrates it. Someone who has built a scheduler and thought about lab processes will have a faster and better-informed intuition about that than I do — and if the answer is no, that is worth knowing now rather than after another year of integration.
